## Solutions
**Q1 — (B).** The 42nd Amendment (1976), often called the "mini-Constitution", added *Socialist*, *Secular* and *Integrity* ("unity of the Nation" became "unity and integrity of the Nation"). It is the only amendment so far to change the Preamble. *Also remember:* "Sovereign", "Democratic" and "Republic" were in the original 1949 text.

**Q2 — (D).** Golaknath (1967) → Kesavananda Bharati (1973) → Maneka Gandhi (1978) → Minerva Mills (1980). *Also remember:* Kesavananda (13-judge bench, the largest ever) laid down the **basic structure** doctrine; Minerva Mills made the balance between FRs and DPSPs and limited judicial review part of the basic structure.

**Q3 — (C).** A is correct: the Kesavananda Bharati case (1973) held the Preamble to be part of the Constitution and amendable within the basic structure limit, and it was amended in 1976. R is false: in Berubari (1960) the Court said the Preamble is **not** a part of the Constitution. *Also remember:* LIC of India case (1995) again held the Preamble to be an integral part of the Constitution.

**Q4 — (A).** DPSP – Ireland; Fundamental Duties – former USSR; "procedure established by law" – Japan; amendment procedure and election of Rajya Sabha members – South Africa. *Also remember:* the Government of India Act, 1935 is the single largest source (federal scheme, office of Governor, emergency provisions, PSCs, judiciary).

**Q5 — (C).** All three are correct. Property is now a constitutional/legal right under Article 300A; Article 21A came with the 86th Amendment (2002) and the RTE Act, 2009 operationalised it; the 44th Amendment made Articles 20 and 21 non-suspendable during emergency. *Also remember:* the same 86th Amendment changed Article 45 (early childhood care up to age 6) and added Fundamental Duty 51A(k).

**Q6 — (C).** Article 32 (right to constitutional remedies) lets a person approach the Supreme Court directly for enforcement of FRs. *Also remember:* High Courts issue writs under Article 226, and their writ power is wider (FRs *and* "any other purpose").

**Q7 — (D).** Article 50 is about **separation of the judiciary from the executive**. International peace and security is **Article 51**. *Also remember:* Article 48A (environment) was inserted by the 42nd Amendment; Article 48 deals with organisation of agriculture and animal husbandry, including prohibiting cow slaughter.

**Q8 — (A).** The Swaran Singh Committee (1976) recommended Fundamental Duties; the 42nd Amendment added Part IVA, Article 51A. *Also remember:* the committee suggested 8 duties, but 10 were added; the idea was borrowed from the former USSR constitution.

**Q9 — (B).** Statement 2 is wrong: Fundamental Duties are **non-justiciable** (no direct writ for their enforcement), though courts use them to interpret laws. Statements 1 and 3 are correct. *Also remember:* the 11th duty, 51A(k), requires a parent/guardian to provide opportunities for education to a child between 6 and 14 years.

**Q10 — (B).** Both statements come from Article 37. R does not explain why DPSPs are non-enforceable; it says why they still matter despite being non-enforceable. *Also remember:* DPSPs are borrowed from the Irish Constitution, and Dr. Ambedkar called them a "novel feature" of the Constitution.

**Q11 — (A).** Nehru moved the Objectives Resolution on **13 December 1946**; it was adopted on **22 January 1947**. *Also remember:* the first meeting of the Constituent Assembly was on 9 December 1946 (Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha as interim President); the Constitution was adopted on 26 November 1949 (now Constitution Day) and came into force on 26 January 1950.

**Q12 — (D).** *Quo warranto* ("by what warrant") tests the legal right of a person to hold a public office. *Also remember:* it can be sought by any person, not only the aggrieved one; it does not lie against a purely private office. Habeas corpus = produce the detained body; mandamus = "we command" a public authority to do its duty; certiorari = quash an order already passed by a lower court/tribunal.
