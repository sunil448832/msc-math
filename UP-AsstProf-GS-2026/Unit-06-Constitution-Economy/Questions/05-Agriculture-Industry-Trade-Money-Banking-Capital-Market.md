## Solutions
**Q1 — (B).** M.S. Swaminathan led the introduction of high-yielding wheat varieties in India in the mid-1960s. *Also remember:* Norman Borlaug (Nobel Peace Prize 1970) is the father of the Green Revolution worldwide; Verghese Kurien is the father of the White Revolution (Operation Flood, 1970).

**Q2 — (C).** The CACP (set up in 1965 as the Agricultural Prices Commission, renamed in 1985) recommends MSPs; the Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs approves them. *Also remember:* MSP is announced for 22 mandated crops; for sugarcane the Centre fixes the Fair and Remunerative Price (FRP), and UP additionally announces a State Advised Price (SAP).

**Q3 — (B).** 1948 – mixed economy; 1956 – Schedules A (17 industries reserved for the State), B and C, called the "economic constitution of India"; 1991 – LPG and delicensing; Competition Act 2002 – replaced the MRTP Act, 1969. *Also remember:* FERA (1973) was replaced by FEMA (1999).

**Q4 — (B).** Statements 1 and 2 are correct. M4 is the **least** liquid; M1 (narrow money) is the most liquid. *Also remember:* M3 = M1 + time deposits with banks; M2 and M4 add post office savings deposits and total post office deposits respectively.

**Q5 — (D).** On 7 October 2026 the MPC unanimously raised the repo rate by 25 basis points, from 5.25% to **5.50%**, and changed its stance from neutral to "calibrated tightening" — its first hike since February 2023. *Also remember (as of October 2026):* SDF 5.25%, MSF and Bank Rate 5.75%, CRR 3%, SLR 18%; Governor Sanjay Malhotra chairs the six-member MPC.

**Q6 — (A).** Because CRR money is locked with the RBI and cannot be lent, a higher CRR leaves banks with less to lend. *Also remember:* CRR is set under Section 42 of the RBI Act, 1934; SLR (liquid assets kept by the bank itself, such as government securities, gold and cash) is under Section 24 of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949.

**Q7 — (D).** RBI (1 April 1935) → SBI (1955, from the Imperial Bank of India) → nationalisation of 14 banks (19 July 1969) → NABARD (1982). *Also remember:* 6 more banks were nationalised in 1980; RBI itself was nationalised on 1 January 1949.

**Q8 — (A).** Prathama Bank, sponsored by Syndicate Bank, was set up at **Moradabad (UP)**; it was among the first five RRBs set up on 2 October 1975. *Also remember:* RRBs were set up on the recommendation of the Narasimham Working Group (1975) and are owned by the Centre (50%), sponsor bank (35%) and State Government (15%).

**Q9 — (C).** The **Sensex** is the index of **30** stocks of the **BSE** (base 1978-79 = 100). The NSE's 50-stock index is the **Nifty 50**. *Also remember:* SEBI was set up in 1988 and became statutory under the SEBI Act, 1992; SIDBI (1990) has its head office in Lucknow; Tuhin Kanta Pandey is the SEBI Chairman as of October 2026.

**Q10 — (A).** The ₹1 note is issued by the Government of India (Ministry of Finance) and signed by the **Finance Secretary**; all other notes are issued by the RBI and signed by the RBI Governor. *Also remember:* coins are minted by the Government; the ₹ symbol, designed by D. Udaya Kumar, was adopted in 2010.

**Q11 — (C).** A higher repo rate makes borrowing costlier, and selling securities in OMO absorbs liquidity from the market; both tighten money supply. Buying securities (B) injects liquidity, and the other options are easing measures. *Also remember:* the inflation target is CPI 4% with a band of 2–6%, retained by the Government for April 2026–March 2031.

**Q12 — (D).** The India–UK CETA was signed on 24 July 2025 and entered into force on **15 July 2026**. *Also remember (as of October 2026):* the India–EFTA TEPA came into force on 1 October 2025 (with a US$100 billion investment commitment over 15 years); India–EU FTA negotiations were concluded on 27 January 2026, but the agreement is not yet in force.
