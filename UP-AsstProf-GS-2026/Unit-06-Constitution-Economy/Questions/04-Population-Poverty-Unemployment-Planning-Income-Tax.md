## Solutions
**Q1 — (C).** Census 2011 sex ratio = 943 (2001: 933; 1991: 927). Child sex ratio (0–6) fell to 919. *Also remember:* Kerala had the highest sex ratio (1084), Haryana the lowest (879).

**Q2 — (B).** UP is the most populous State (about 19.98 crore, roughly 16.5% of India) and Bihar has the highest density (1106 per sq km). Statement 2 is wrong: UP's sex ratio is **912**, below the national 943. *Also remember:* UP literacy was 67.7% against the national 74.04%; UP density was 829.

**Q3 — (D).** The reference date is 00:00 hours on **1 March 2027**; for snow-bound areas (Ladakh and non-synchronous snow-bound areas of J&K, Himachal Pradesh and Uttarakhand) it is 1 October 2026. *Also remember (status as of October 2026):* Phase I, houselisting and housing census, ran from April to September 2026 (each State/UT in a 30-day window, with a self-enumeration option); Phase II, population enumeration, is due in February 2027. It is India's first digital census and will include caste enumeration, the first since 1931. It will be the 16th Census and the 8th since Independence.

**Q4 — (D).** Lakdawala – State-specific lines with CPI-AL (rural)/CPI-IW (urban); Tendulkar – dropped calorie anchoring, MRP basis, 21.9% poverty in 2011-12; Rangarajan – 29.5% in 2011-12; Alagh – 2400/2100 calorie norm. *Also remember:* the Rangarajan line was about ₹32/day (rural) and ₹47/day (urban) per person for 2011-12.

**Q5 — (A).** Too many family members work on a small farm; removing some does not cut output because their marginal product is about zero. That explains why it is common in agriculture. *Also remember:* **seasonal** unemployment (idle months between sowing and harvest) is the other typical rural type; **frictional** = between jobs; **structural** = mismatch of skills and jobs.

**Q6 — (C).** Second Plan (1956–61) → Plan Holiday (1966–69, three Annual Plans) → Rolling Plan (1978–80, Janata govt) → Eighth Plan (1992–97, after the 1991 reforms). *Also remember:* the Fifth Plan (1974–79) was cut short in 1978; the Twelfth Plan (2012–17) was the last Five-Year Plan.

**Q7 — (B).** NITI Aayog (National Institution for Transforming India) was set up by a Cabinet resolution on **1 January 2015**; the Planning Commission had been set up on 15 March 1950. *Also remember:* both are non-constitutional, non-statutory bodies chaired by the Prime Minister; NITI's Governing Council includes all Chief Ministers and Lt. Governors.

**Q8 — (C).** The Act received Presidential assent on 21 August 2025 and came into force on **1 April 2026**; it has 536 sections, 23 chapters and 16 schedules (the 1961 Act had 819 sections and 47 chapters). *Also remember:* "tax year" (12 months from 1 April) replaces the old "previous year / assessment year" pair; the Income-tax Rules, 2026 were notified in March 2026.

**Q9 — (A).** Taxable income = 12,50,000 − 75,000 (standard deduction) = ₹11,75,000. Tax on slabs would be ₹57,500 (5% of 4–8 lakh = 20,000; 10% of 8–11.75 lakh = 37,500), but the rebate makes tax **nil** when taxable income does not exceed ₹12 lakh (maximum rebate ₹60,000). Slabs as of October 2026 (unchanged in Budget 2026). *Also remember:* so a salaried person pays nil tax up to ₹12.75 lakh of salary under the new regime; Health and Education Cess is 4%.

**Q10 — (D).** Income tax is levied and collected by the **Union** (Union List entry 82: taxes on income other than agricultural income) and shared with States through the Finance Commission (Art 270). Agricultural income is taxed only by States (State List entry 46). *Also remember:* CBDT was constituted under the Central Boards of Revenue Act, 1963.

**Q11 — (A).** Statements 1 and 2 are correct. Statement 3 is wrong: **Uttar Pradesh** recorded the largest decline (about 5.94 crore), followed by Bihar (3.77 crore). National multidimensional poverty fell from 29.17% (2013-14) to 11.28% (2022-23). *Also remember:* the global MPI is prepared by UNDP and OPHI (Oxford); India's national MPI adds two indicators, maternal health and bank account, to the global 10.

**Q12 — (B).** Monthly and quarterly PLFS estimates use the **Current Weekly Status (CWS)** — reference period of the last 7 days. As of October 2026, the September 2026 bulletin (released 15 October 2026) put the unemployment rate (age 15+, CWS) at 5.0%. *Also remember:* annual PLFS reports also give Usual Status (reference period 365 days); PLFS was launched by the NSO in 2017.
