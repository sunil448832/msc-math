### Preamble
| Item | Fact |
|---|---|
| Nature of State | **S**overeign **S**ocialist **S**ecular **D**emocratic **R**epublic |
| Objectives | **J**ustice (social, economic, political), **L**iberty (thought, expression, belief, faith, worship), **E**quality (status, opportunity), **F**raternity (dignity of individual, unity and integrity of Nation) |
| Source of authority | "We, the people of India" |
| Amended | Only once, 42nd Amendment 1976 (Socialist, Secular, Integrity) |
| Part of Constitution? | Berubari 1960: **No** → Kesavananda 1973: **Yes** → LIC 1995: Yes |
| Justiciable? | No. Used only to interpret ambiguous provisions |
| Ideals of Liberty, Equality, Fraternity | From the French Revolution |

### Fundamental Rights (Part III, Art 12–35)
| Group | Articles | Key points |
|---|---|---|
| Right to Equality | 14–18 | 14 equality before law; 15 no discrimination; 16 equal opportunity in public jobs; 17 untouchability abolished; 18 titles abolished |
| Right to Freedom | 19–22 | 19: six freedoms (property 19(1)(f) deleted); 20 protection in conviction (no ex-post-facto law, no double jeopardy, no self-incrimination); 21 life and personal liberty; 21A education 6–14; 22 arrest and detention |
| Against Exploitation | 23–24 | 23 traffic in humans, begar; 24 no child below 14 in factories/mines/hazardous work |
| Freedom of Religion | 25–28 | |
| Cultural and Educational | 29–30 | Minorities' right to run institutions (30) |
| Constitutional Remedies | 32 | 5 writs; "heart and soul" (Ambedkar) |

- Originally **7** FRs; right to property (Art 31) removed by **44th Amendment 1978** → now **Art 300A** (legal right). Now **6**.
- Available **only to citizens**: Art 15, 16, 19, 29, 30. The others (14, 20, 21, 21A, 22, 23–28) also cover foreigners.
- Art 20 and 21 **cannot be suspended** even in National Emergency (44th Amendment).

### Writs
| Writ | Meaning | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Habeas corpus | "To have the body" | Against illegal detention (public or private) |
| Mandamus | "We command" | Public authority to perform a public duty (not against President/Governor or private person) |
| Prohibition | "To forbid" | Stops a lower court/tribunal *before* it decides (preventive) |
| Certiorari | "To be certified" | Quashes an order of a lower court/tribunal *already passed* (curative) |
| Quo warranto | "By what authority" | Tests the right to hold a public office |

### Fundamental Duties (Part IVA, Art 51A)
- Swaran Singh Committee (1976) → **42nd Amendment** added **10** duties; **86th Amendment (2002)** added the **11th**: 51A(k) parents/guardians to give education opportunities to children 6–14.
- Source: former USSR. Non-justiciable. Apply to citizens only.
- Justice J.S. Verma Committee (1999) suggested ways to operationalise them.

## 2. Memory hooks
1. **Cricket analogy.** The Constitution is the rule-book of a cricket match. FRs are the player's rights that the umpire (court) enforces on the spot. DPSPs are the coach's advice: no umpire can enforce them, but a good team follows them. Fundamental Duties are the spirit-of-cricket code the players are expected to honour.
2. **"SSSDR" → "Sab Saath, Sab Desh Rakshak"** for Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic, Republic. Ideals: **JLEF** = "**J**ust **L**ove **E**very **F**riend" (Justice, Liberty, Equality, Fraternity).
3. **42nd Amendment = "SSI"**, like the small-scale industry tag: **S**ocialist, **S**ecular, **I**ntegrity were added to the Preamble.
4. **Number trick for duties: 42 + 10, 86 + 1.** The 42nd Amendment gave 10 duties; the 86th gave 1 more (education), and the same 86th gave Art 21A. "86 = education amendment."
5. **Writs as a hospital.** Habeas corpus = "bring the patient (body) out"; Mandamus = "doctor, do your duty"; Prohibition = "stop the operation before it starts"; Certiorari = "cancel the operation report already written"; Quo warranto = "show your medical degree".
6. **Case timeline "G-K-M-M" = "Good Kids Make Merit"** for Golaknath 1967 → Kesavananda 1973 → Maneka 1978 → Minerva 1980.
7. **Sources by country, "IRE-DIRECTS".** IREland gives DIRECTive principles. "**U**SSR **D**uties": Soviets loved duties. "**J**apan **P**rocedure": Japanese trains run strictly by procedure. "**S**outh **A**frica **A**mends".
8. **Article 50 vs 51.** "50 = fifty-fifty split of power (judiciary separated from executive); 51 = one world (international peace)."
9. **Art 32 = "32 teeth"**: a Constitution without Art 32 would have no teeth to bite violators; that is why it is its "heart and soul".
10. **Gandhian DPSPs = "40-43-46-47-48".** Picture Gandhiji walking through a village (40 panchayat) with a charkha (43 cottage industry), helping the weak (46), banning liquor (47), and protecting cows (48).

## 3. Don't mix up
| Item | vs | Item |
|---|---|---|
| Berubari 1960: Preamble **not** part | | Kesavananda 1973 / LIC 1995: Preamble **is** part |
| Art 32: SC writs, only for FRs; itself an FR | | Art 226: HC writs, for FRs and other legal rights; not an FR |
| Prohibition: before decision | | Certiorari: after decision |
| Art 45 now: ECCE up to 6 years | | Art 21A: free education 6–14 years (FR) |
| Golaknath: cannot amend FRs | | Kesavananda: can amend, not the basic structure |
| Objectives Resolution moved 13 Dec 1946 | | Adopted 22 Jan 1947 |
| Swaran Singh Committee: Fundamental Duties | | Sardar Patel Committee: Advisory Committee on FRs and minorities in the Constituent Assembly |
| Art 300A: property as constitutional right | | Art 31 (old): property as FR, deleted |

## 4. Exam traps
- The Preamble has been amended **once**, not "never".
- Right to property is **still a constitutional right** (Art 300A), just not a Fundamental Right.
- DPSPs are "fundamental in governance" (Art 37) but **not enforceable**. Do not confuse "fundamental" with "justiciable".
- Habeas corpus lies even against **private** persons; mandamus does **not**.
- During National Emergency, Art 19 is suspended automatically only on the ground of **war or external aggression**, not armed rebellion; Art 20 and 21 are never suspended.
- 11 Fundamental Duties, not 10: the 11th came in 2002.
- "Integrity" (not "unity") was the word added in 1976.
