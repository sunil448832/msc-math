## Solutions
**Q1 — (B).** Michael Scriven introduced "formative" and "summative" evaluation in 1967, in the context of curriculum evaluation. *Also remember:* Bloom applied the idea to classroom learning through mastery learning.

**Q2 — (C).** CCE is continuous (spread across the year) and comprehensive (scholastic plus co-scholastic), so statement 3 is false. *Also remember:* Section 29(2)(h) of the RTE Act provides for comprehensive and continuous evaluation. CBSE ran the CCE scheme from 2009–10 and later withdrew it.

**Q3 — (A).** A bathroom scale that always reads 2 kg extra is consistent (reliable) but wrong (not valid). R explains A. *Also remember:* validity requires reliability, but reliability does not guarantee validity.

**Q4 — (D).** Placement comes before instruction, formative during it, diagnostic for persistent problems, and summative at the end. *Also remember:* diagnostic testing is followed by remedial teaching.

**Q5 — (A).** Expert judgement of content checks content validity, not reliability. *Also remember:* other reliability measures are KR-20, Cronbach's alpha (internal consistency) and the Spearman–Brown correction for split-half.

**Q6 — (B).** A criterion-referenced test compares each learner against a fixed standard (mastery). *Also remember:* Robert Glaser popularised the term "criterion-referenced". A norm-referenced test ranks learners against a group (percentiles).

**Q7 — (C).** p = (24 + 12) / 60 = 0.60. D = (24 − 12) / 30 = 0.40. *Also remember:* D ≥ 0.40 is usually treated as a very good item, and a negative D signals a faulty item. A p-value around 0.5 indicates moderate difficulty.

**Q8 — (D).** Dale's cone runs from direct, purposeful experiences at the base to verbal symbols at the apex, the most abstract. *Also remember:* Dale proposed the cone in 1946. The popular "we remember 10% of what we read…" percentages are NOT part of Dale's original work.

**Q9 — (C).** In the Pygmalion effect, a teacher's expectations become a self-fulfilling prophecy (Rosenthal & Jacobson, 1968). *Also remember:* the Hawthorne effect is a change in behaviour because people know they are being observed.

**Q10 — (A).** Subject mastery and communication are teacher-related factors, not institutional ones. *Also remember:* institutional factors include infrastructure, administration, library and ICT facilities, and academic culture.

**Q11 — (B).** In the halo effect, one irrelevant favourable trait (neatness) colours the whole judgement. A rubric and anonymous scripts reduce it. *Also remember:* "generosity" or "leniency" error means marking everyone high. "Central tendency" error means avoiding extreme marks.

**Q12 — (D).** Timely corrective feedback during a course comes from **formative** evaluation, so A is false. R is true. *Also remember:* "assessment FOR learning" is formative and "assessment OF learning" is summative.
