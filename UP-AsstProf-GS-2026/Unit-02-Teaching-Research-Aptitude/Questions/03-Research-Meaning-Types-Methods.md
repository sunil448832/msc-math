## Solutions
**Q1 — (B).** Kurt Lewin coined "action research" in the 1940s. *Also remember:* Stephen M. Corey brought it into education ("Action Research to Improve School Practices", 1953).

**Q2 — (C).** Action research is practitioner-led, local, aimed at solving an immediate problem, and cyclical (plan → act → observe → reflect). *Also remember:* its results are not meant for wide generalisation.

**Q3 — (A).** Experimental research manipulates, historical research criticises sources, ex post facto research traces cause after the effect, and ethnography relies on immersion. *Also remember:* ex post facto is also called causal-comparative research. The researcher cannot manipulate the IV, for example in a study of smoking and cancer.

**Q4 — (D).** A stricter α (0.01) **reduces** the chance of a Type I error, though it raises the chance of a Type II error. *Also remember:* power of a test = 1 − β.

**Q5 — (C).** R describes **internal** criticism (credibility of content). External criticism checks genuineness: author, date, forgery. *Also remember:* primary sources (original documents, eyewitnesses) are preferred over secondary ones in historical research.

**Q6 — (B).** The sequence is Problem → Literature review → Hypothesis → Design and data collection → Analysis → Interpretation and report. *Also remember:* the literature review also helps refine the problem and avoid duplication.

**Q7 — (A).** Anxiety is the measured outcome (DV). Uncontrolled fitness is an extraneous (possibly confounding) variable. Yoga is the IV. *Also remember:* an intervening variable is an unobservable internal process (such as motivation) that links IV and DV.

**Q8 — (C).** The bands are Level 0 (up to 10%, minor), Level 1 (above 10% to 40%), Level 2 (above 40% to 60%) and Level 3 (above 60%). *Also remember:* for students, Level 3 leads to cancellation of registration for the programme. Common knowledge, references, formulae and generic terms are excluded from similarity checks.

**Q9 — (D).** "viz." (videlicet) means "namely". "For example" is "e.g." *Also remember:* "i.e." means "that is", and "cf." means "compare".

**Q10 — (D).** Re-publishing one's own work as new is self-plagiarism or duplicate publication. *Also remember:* the three classic forms of research misconduct are FFP: Fabrication (making up data), Falsification (manipulating data) and Plagiarism.

**Q11 — (B).** INFLIBNET (Information and Library Network), an Inter-University Centre of UGC at Gandhinagar, runs Shodhganga. *Also remember:* Shodhgangotri hosts synopses of ongoing research, and UGC regulations require theses to be deposited in Shodhganga.

**Q12 — (A).** Randomisation spreads unknown extraneous influences evenly, so the outcome can be attributed to the IV. *Also remember:* internal validity means "did the IV cause the change?" External validity means "can it be generalised?"
