## Solutions
**Q1 — (C).** Reflective (problem-centred) teaching is credited to Bigge and Hunt, usually written simply as "Hunt" in exams. *Also remember:* memory level goes with Herbart and understanding level with Morrison. The order is H-M-H: Herbart, Morrison, Hunt.

**Q2 — (B).** Kilpatrick gave the project method (building on Dewey), Parkhurst the Dalton plan, Armstrong the heuristic ("I find") method, and Skinner programmed instruction (operant conditioning). *Also remember:* the Keller Plan (Personalised System of Instruction) is another individualised method. Brainstorming is credited to Alex Osborn.

**Q3 — (D).** The revised order is Remember → Understand → Apply → Analyse → Evaluate → Create. *Also remember:* in the 2001 revision, Evaluate moved below Create, and "Synthesis" was renamed Create.

**Q4 — (A).** Statement 3 is wrong because reflective teaching presupposes the memory and understanding levels. *Also remember:* Morrison's understanding-level model has five steps: exploration, presentation, assimilation, organisation, recitation.

**Q5 — (C).** The lecture method is economical for large groups, but it is teacher-centred and the learners are often passive. *Also remember:* to make a lecture interactive, add questioning, think-pair-share or short buzz groups.

**Q6 — (C).** The correct maxim runs from simple to complex. *Also remember:* other maxims are whole to part, analysis to synthesis, induction to deduction, empirical to rational, and psychological to logical.

**Q7 — (B).** Students who recall formulas but cannot use them in new situations are stuck below "Apply". *Also remember:* Apply means using a procedure in a new situation, which is where transfer of learning gets tested.

**Q8 — (B).** Identifying a problem, forming hypotheses and testing them is the reflective level of teaching. *Also remember:* at the reflective level the classroom is open and learner-centred, and essay or open-ended evaluation fits best.

**Q9 — (A).** Micro-teaching was developed at Stanford University in the early 1960s, associated with Dwight W. Allen. *Also remember:* the cycle is Plan → Teach → Feedback → Re-plan → Re-teach → Re-feedback. The common Indian model takes 36 minutes (6+6+12+6+6), with 5–10 pupils and a single skill.

**Q10 — (D).** All three statements are true. *Also remember:* for the psychomotor domain, the taxonomies of Simpson, Dave (imitation → naturalisation) and Harrow are the ones usually asked.

**Q11 — (D).** Andragogy assumes adults are self-directed, bring experience, and are problem-centred and internally motivated. *Also remember:* pedagogy is teaching children and andragogy is teaching adults (Knowles). Heutagogy is self-determined learning.

**Q12 — (A).** Adams saw teaching as bipolar (teacher and learner). Dewey added the social environment, which makes it tripolar. *Also remember:* teaching is called both an art and a science, and its central objective is a desirable change in the learner's behaviour.
