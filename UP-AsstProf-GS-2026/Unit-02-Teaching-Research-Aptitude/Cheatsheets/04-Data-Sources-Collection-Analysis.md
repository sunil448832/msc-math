# Memory Sheet · Unit 2 · Topic 04 — Data: Sources, Collection and Analysis
> Questions: [../Questions/04-Data-Sources-Collection-Analysis.md](../Questions/04-Data-Sources-Collection-Analysis.md)

## 1. Core facts

### Sources
| Primary | Secondary |
|---|---|
| Collected first-hand for the present study | Already collected by someone else |
| Questionnaire, schedule, interview, observation, experiment | Census reports, NSS/NSO reports, RBI bulletins, journals, theses, archives |
| Original, costly, time-consuming | Cheap and quick, but check suitability, reliability and adequacy |

### Tools of data collection
| Tool | Key point |
|---|---|
| Questionnaire | Self-administered, can be mailed or online, needs literate respondents |
| Schedule | Filled in by the enumerator face to face |
| Interview | Structured (fixed questions) vs unstructured/in-depth; focus-group interview |
| Observation | Participant vs non-participant; structured vs unstructured |
| Rating scales | Likert (summated, 1932), Thurstone (equal-appearing intervals), Guttman (cumulative/scalogram), Osgood (semantic differential), Bogardus (social distance) |
| Sociometry | Moreno; sociogram of peer choices |

### Sampling
| Probability (known chance) | Non-probability |
|---|---|
| Simple random (lottery, random numbers) | Convenience/accidental |
| Systematic (every k-th) | Purposive/judgemental |
| Stratified (homogeneous strata, random from each) | Quota (strata plus the interviewer's choice) |
| Cluster (whole groups chosen at random) | Snowball (referral chain, for hidden populations) |
| Multistage | — |

### Levels of measurement: NOIR
| Scale | Property | Example | Average | Tests |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nominal | Labels | Gender, roll no., religion | Mode | Chi-square |
| Ordinal | Order | Ranks, grades | Median | Rank correlation (Spearman) |
| Interval | Equal gaps, no true zero | °C, IQ, calendar year | Mean | t, F, Pearson r |
| Ratio | True zero | Height, weight, income, age | Mean (also GM, HM) | All |

### Descriptive statistics
- Central tendency: mean, median, mode. **Mode = 3 Median − 2 Mean** (for moderately skewed data).
- Dispersion: range, quartile deviation, mean deviation, SD, variance; CV = SD/Mean × 100.
- SD is unaffected by a change of origin (adding a constant) and affected by a change of scale (multiplying).
- Skewness: positive means Mean > Median > Mode (tail to the right). Negative is the reverse.
- Correlation r ranges from −1 to +1.
- Pie chart: 100% = 360°, so 1% = 3.6°.

## 2. Memory hooks
1. **NOIR is French for "black"**: Nominal → Ordinal → Interval → Ratio, each step adding one power. **"Name → Order → Interval gap → Real zero."**
2. **Cricket team analogy for NOIR.** Jersey numbers are nominal (Dhoni's 7 is not "better" than 6). Batting order is ordinal. Temperature at Green Park, Kanpur in °C is interval (0 °C does not mean no heat). Runs scored is ratio (0 runs means none, and 100 is twice 50).
3. **Questionnaire vs schedule is like a self-service vs a waiter-served restaurant.** With a questionnaire the respondent fills it in. With a schedule the enumerator (the waiter) writes it down for you.
4. **Stratified vs cluster is like a thali vs a whole dish.** Stratified takes a spoonful from **every** bowl (stratum). Cluster picks a **few whole** dishes at random.
5. **Snowball is a WhatsApp forward chain**: one respondent refers the next. It suits drug users, migrant workers and other hidden groups.
6. **"3 Med – 2 Mean"**: "three Medicines minus two Meals". Mode = 3Md − 2M.
7. **Skewness follows the tail.** The mean is pulled toward the tail, like a dog on a leash. Tail to the right (positive) means the mean is largest.
8. **Pie trick: "36 degrees per 10%."** 45% = 4.5 × 36 = 162°.
9. **Scale developers: "Likert Sums, Thurstone Equals, Guttman Accumulates, Osgood Differentiates, Bogardus Distances"** (LS-TE-GA-OD-BD).
10. **Primary vs secondary is home-cooked vs a packed meal.** Census data is home-cooked for the Census office and a packed meal for the researcher.

## 3. Don't mix up
| This | Not this |
|---|---|
| Stratified (random within strata) — probability | Quota (interviewer choice within strata) — non-probability |
| Cluster (whole groups) | Stratified (part of every group) |
| Interval (no true zero, e.g. °C) | Ratio (true zero, e.g. kelvin, weight) |
| Questionnaire — self-filled | Schedule — enumerator-filled |
| Bogardus — social distance | Guttman — cumulative scalogram |
| Likert — summated | Thurstone — equal-appearing intervals |
| Positive skew: Mean > Median > Mode | Negative skew: Mean < Median < Mode |

## 4. Exam traps
- °C and IQ are **interval**, not ratio. 40 °C is not "twice as hot" as 20 °C.
- Quota sampling looks like stratified sampling but is **non-probability**.
- r can **never** exceed ±1.
- Percentage change always uses the **earlier year** as the base. The same absolute increase gives a smaller percentage on a larger base.
- In averaging questions, recompute the total first: new mean = (old total ± change) ÷ n.
- Data is "secondary" relative to the **user**. The same data can be primary for its collector.
