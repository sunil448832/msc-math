# Memory Sheet · Unit 2 · Topic 01 — Teaching: Nature, Objectives, Levels and Methods

## 1. Core facts

### Nature and objectives of teaching
| Point | Key fact |
|---|---|
| Central aim | A desirable change in the learner's behaviour (cognitive, affective, psychomotor) |
| Bipolar view | Adams: teacher ↔ learner |
| Tripolar view | John Dewey: teacher, learner and social environment/curriculum |
| Nature | Both an art and a science. It is interactive, purposeful and goal-directed, and it can be observed, analysed and improved |
| Need | Guided learning, transfer of culture, developing thinking, preparing citizens and workforce |

### Levels of teaching (H-M-H)
| Level | Proponent | Focus | Who dominates | Evaluation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Memory | **Herbart** | Rote recall of facts | Teacher | Oral/written recall, objective tests |
| Understanding | **Morrison** | Insight, mastery, seeing relationships | Teacher and learner | Objective plus short-answer |
| Reflective | **Bigge & Hunt** | Problem-solving, critical thinking | Learner | Essay, open-ended, problem tasks |

- Herbart's 5 formal steps: Preparation → Presentation → Comparison/Association → Generalisation → Application
- Morrison's 5 steps: Exploration → Presentation → Assimilation → Organisation → Recitation

### Bloom's taxonomy
| Domain | Who | Levels (low → high) |
|---|---|---|
| Cognitive (1956) | Bloom et al. | Knowledge, Comprehension, Application, Analysis, Synthesis, Evaluation |
| Cognitive, revised (2001) | Anderson & Krathwohl | Remember, Understand, Apply, Analyse, Evaluate, **Create** |
| Affective (1964) | Krathwohl, Bloom, Masia | Receiving, Responding, Valuing, Organisation, Characterisation |
| Psychomotor | Simpson / Dave / Harrow | Dave: Imitation, Manipulation, Precision, Articulation, Naturalisation |

### Methods
| Teacher-centred | Learner-centred |
|---|---|
| Lecture, demonstration, team teaching, recitation | Project (Kilpatrick), Dalton plan (Parkhurst), heuristic (Armstrong), discovery (Bruner), problem-solving, case study, brainstorming (Osborn), role-play, programmed instruction (Skinner), Keller Plan/PSI, Montessori, flipped classroom, Socratic questioning |

### Maxims of teaching
Known → unknown · Simple → complex · Concrete → abstract · Particular → general · Whole → part · Analysis → synthesis · Induction → deduction · Empirical → rational · Psychological → logical

### Learner characteristics
| Idea | Name |
|---|---|
| Andragogy (adult learning) | Malcolm Knowles |
| Multiple intelligences (1983) | Howard Gardner |
| VARK learning styles | Neil Fleming |
| Zone of Proximal Development, scaffolding | Vygotsky |
| Stages of cognitive development | Piaget |
| Adolescent and adult learners | Self-directed, experience-rich, problem-centred; individual differences matter |

## 2. Memory hooks
1. **H-M-H, a staircase.** Herbart on the ground floor (memory), Morrison on the first floor (understanding), Hunt on the terrace (reflection). You cannot reach the terrace without climbing the lower floors, so reflective teaching presupposes the other two.
2. **"Remember Understanding Applies to Analysing, Evaluating and Creating" (RUAAEC).** For the revised Bloom's list, picture a cooking class: read the recipe (Remember), know why it works (Understand), cook it (Apply), taste and break down the flavours (Analyse), judge it (Evaluate), invent your own dish (Create).
3. **Affective "RRVOC": "Ram Recently Valued Our Culture"**, for Receiving, Responding, Valuing, Organisation, Characterisation.
4. **Dave's psychomotor "I M P A N"**, like learning to drive. You Imitate the instructor, Manipulate the gears yourself, become Precise, Articulate (coordinate) clutch and brake, and finally drive Naturally.
5. **Method–person pairs.** **K**ilpatrick **P**roject = "KP" like a KPI project. **P**arkhurst **D**alton = "P-D", Parkhurst Dalton. **A**rmstrong **H**euristic = "Armstrong finds (heurisko) things on the moon". **S**kinner **P**rogrammed = the Skinner box is a programmed machine.
6. **Tripolar Dewey is a three-legged stool**: teacher, learner, society. Remove one and the stool falls. Adams's bipolar view is a seesaw with two people only.
7. **Micro-teaching 36 minutes, "6-6-12-6-6"**: teach, feedback, a long tea break to re-plan, re-teach, re-feedback. Stanford is where it began.
8. **Maxims always move from the comfortable to the challenging**, like climbing from the plains of Lucknow to the hills. If an option reverses this (complex → simple, abstract → concrete), it is the trap.
9. **Andragogy is the adult's "WIIFM" (What's In It For Me?).** Adults learn when content touches their job and life.

## 3. Don't mix up
| This | Not this |
|---|---|
| Memory level — Herbart | Understanding level — Morrison |
| Reflective level — Hunt | Programmed instruction — Skinner |
| Project method — Kilpatrick | Dalton plan — Parkhurst |
| Heuristic — Armstrong | Discovery learning — Bruner |
| Bipolar — Adams | Tripolar — Dewey |
| Original Bloom top = Evaluation | Revised Bloom top = Create |
| Affective domain — Krathwohl | Psychomotor — Simpson/Dave/Harrow |
| Pedagogy — children | Andragogy — adults (Knowles) |

## 4. Exam traps
- "Reflective level can be achieved directly" is **false**.
- Synthesis (old) became **Create** (new) and moved to the very top. Evaluation moved below it.
- The lecture method suits large classes but is **not** participatory. Any option that claims the lecture method ensures participation is wrong.
- In scenario questions, the best answer is usually the one that is **learner-centred, activity-based and fitted to the level of the objective**. Avoid "punish", "ignore", "repeat the same lecture" and "complain to the principal".
- When students recall but cannot use what they know, the gap is at **Apply**. When they use it but cannot judge or justify, the gap is at **Evaluate**.
