## Solutions
**Q1 — (B).** The Stockholm Conference opened on 5 June 1972. It also created UNEP (headquarters Nairobi). *Also remember:* Indira Gandhi addressed the conference, describing poverty as the "greatest polluter".

**Q2 — (A).** UNFCCC and CBD were opened for signature at Rio (with Agenda 21, the Rio Declaration and the Forest Principles). The Kyoto Protocol was adopted at COP3 in 1997. *Also remember:* the UNCCD (1994) was negotiated as a follow-up to Rio; Rio+20 (2012) launched the process that led to the SDGs.

**Q3 — (C).** Basel (1989) = hazardous wastes; Rotterdam (1998) = PIC; Stockholm (2001) = POPs; Minamata (2013) = mercury. *Also remember:* Basel, Rotterdam and Stockholm share a joint secretariat ("BRS conventions").

**Q4 — (D).** Ramsar (1971) → Montreal (1987) → Kyoto (1997) → Paris (2015). *Also remember:* Kyoto came into force in 2005 and Paris on 4 November 2016.

**Q5 — (B).** HFCs do not harm ozone but are powerful greenhouse gases; the Kigali Amendment was adopted in October 2016 in Rwanda. *Also remember:* the Montreal Protocol is the first UN treaty to be ratified by every UN member state.

**Q6 — (B).** Both statements are true, but R describes Kyoto's design, not why Paris uses NDCs. Paris used a "bottom-up" approach so that all countries, developed and developing, would take part. *Also remember:* the Paris goal is to keep warming "well below 2 °C" and to pursue efforts to limit it to 1.5 °C. NDCs are updated every 5 years.

**Q7 — (A).** "Mutirão" is a Tupi-Guarani word for collective community effort. The decision did not include a fossil-fuel roadmap; it called for tripling adaptation finance by 2035 and set up the Belém Action Mechanism for a just transition (as of November 2025). *Also remember:* the UAE Consensus (COP28, Dubai, 2023) first called for "transitioning away from fossil fuels" in energy systems.

**Q8 — (D).** All three are correct as of November 2026. Türkiye hosts COP31 (Antalya, 9–20 November 2026), and Australia's Chris Bowen is "President of Negotiations". Brazil launched the TFFF (a fund to pay tropical countries to keep forests standing) at the Belém summit in November 2025. *Also remember:* COP29 (Baku, 2024) set the New Collective Quantified Goal of at least USD 300 billion a year by 2035.

**Q9 — (C).** The National Green Hydrogen Mission was approved in 2023 and is not one of the NAPCC missions. The original 8 are Solar, Enhanced Energy Efficiency, Sustainable Habitat, Water, Sustaining the Himalayan Ecosystem, Green India, Sustainable Agriculture and Strategic Knowledge for Climate Change. *Also remember:* the NAPCC was released on 30 June 2008.

**Q10 — (C).** Chipko (1973, Chamoli, then in Uttar Pradesh, now Uttarakhand) — Chandi Prasad Bhatt (Sunderlal Bahuguna and Gaura Devi were also leaders); Appiko (1983, Uttara Kannada, Karnataka) — Panduranga Hegde; NBA — Medha Patkar; Navdanya (seed conservation) — Vandana Shiva. *Also remember:* Gaura Devi led the women of Reni village in 1974, and Chipko is a famous UP-era movement.

**Q11 — (A).** LiFE = Lifestyle for Environment. PM Modi first proposed it at COP26 (Glasgow, 2021) and launched it with UN Secretary-General António Guterres at Ekta Nagar (Kevadia), Gujarat, on 20 October 2022. *Also remember:* India's other global initiative is the International Solar Alliance (ISA), launched with France at COP21 Paris (2015); its headquarters is in Gurugram.

**Q12 — (D).** India's NDC for 2031–35 pledges at least a 47% cut in emissions intensity from 2005 levels by 2035. It also targets about 60% of installed electricity capacity from non-fossil sources and a carbon sink of 3.5–4.0 billion tonnes CO₂e through forest and tree cover (as of 2026). *Also remember:* the earlier 2022 NDC targets were 45% intensity reduction and 50% non-fossil capacity by 2030; the net-zero target year is 2070 (announced at COP26).
