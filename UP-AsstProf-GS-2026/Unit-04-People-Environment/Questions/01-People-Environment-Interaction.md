## Solutions
**Q1 — (B).** British ecologist Arthur G. Tansley coined "ecosystem" in 1935. *Also remember:* Charles Elton gave the idea of the food chain, the ecological niche and the pyramid of numbers (*Animal Ecology*, 1927); E.P. Odum is called the "father of modern ecosystem ecology".

**Q2 — (A).** Raymond Lindeman (1942) showed that only about 10% of energy passes from one trophic level to the next. *Also remember:* this is why food chains rarely have more than 4–5 links; Ernst Haeckel coined the word "ecology" (1866).

**Q3 — (D).** All three are the standard Myers / Conservation International criteria. *Also remember:* there are 36 hotspots worldwide; they cover about 2.5% of the land surface yet hold over half of the world's endemic plant species.

**Q4 — (C).** India's four hotspots are the Himalaya, Indo-Burma (North-East), the Western Ghats & Sri Lanka, and Sundaland (Nicobar Islands). The Eastern Ghats is not a hotspot. *Also remember:* India is one of the 17 "megadiverse" countries.

**Q5 — (A).** Because energy is lost at each step (second law of thermodynamics), each higher level has less energy, so the pyramid can never be inverted; R explains A. *Also remember:* the pyramid of biomass can be inverted in aquatic ecosystems, and the pyramid of numbers is inverted in a tree ecosystem.

**Q6 — (C).** A is true (biomagnification). R is false: DDT is fat-soluble (lipophilic) and persistent, so it accumulates in fatty tissue instead of being excreted. *Also remember:* DDT causes thinning of eggshells in birds such as eagles and pelicans, which was highlighted in Rachel Carson's *Silent Spring* (1962).

**Q7 — (B).** Richter = magnitude; Saffir–Simpson = hurricane wind categories 1–5; Enhanced Fujita = tornado (EF0–EF5); Modified Mercalli = intensity (I–XII) based on observed damage. *Also remember:* the instrument that records earthquakes is the seismograph; the point on the surface directly above the focus is the epicentre.

**Q8 — (D).** Stockholm (1972) → Brundtland Report (1987) → Hyogo Framework (2005–2015) → Sendai Framework (2015–2030). *Also remember:* the Sendai Framework has 7 global targets and 4 priorities for action.

**Q9 — (C).** *Our Common Future* (1987) was the report of the World Commission on Environment and Development, chaired by Gro Harlem Brundtland (Norway). *Also remember:* *The Limits to Growth* (1972) was the Club of Rome report; Agenda 21 came from Rio 1992.

**Q10 — (A).** The NDMA is chaired by the Prime Minister and the SDMA by the Chief Minister (not the Governor); the NDRF is constituted under Section 44 of the Act. *Also remember:* a District Disaster Management Authority (DDMA) is chaired by the District Collector/District Magistrate.

**Q11 — (B).** Garrett Hardin's essay "The Tragedy of the Commons" appeared in *Science* in 1968. *Also remember:* Elinor Ostrom (Nobel Prize in Economics, 2009) showed that communities can manage commons sustainably.

**Q12 — (D).** The Nawabganj Bird Sanctuary (now Shaheed Chandra Shekhar Azad Pakshi Vihar) is in Unnao district, on the Lucknow–Kanpur highway. *Also remember:* Dudhwa became a tiger reserve in 1987, and the Sarus crane is the state bird of UP.
