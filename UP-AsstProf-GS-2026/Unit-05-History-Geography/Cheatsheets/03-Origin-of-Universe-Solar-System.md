## 1. Core facts

### Universe and Big Bang
| Item | Fact |
|---|---|
| Age of universe | about **13.8 billion years** |
| Age of Sun / solar system / Earth | about **4.6 billion years** (Earth about 4.54 billion) |
| Big Bang idea | Georges **Lemaître** (1927, "primeval atom") |
| Expanding universe | Edwin **Hubble** (1929): galaxies recede (redshift) |
| CMB discovered | **Penzias & Wilson**, 1965 (Nobel 1978) |
| Rival theory | **Steady State** (Hoyle, Bondi, Gold). Hoyle coined the term "Big Bang" |
| Our galaxy | **Milky Way**, a barred spiral (*Akash Ganga*) |
| Nearest large galaxy | **Andromeda** (spiral; heading towards the Milky Way) |
| Light-year | The distance light travels in one year (a unit of **distance**, not time) |
| Sunlight to Earth | about **8 min 20 s** |
| 1 Astronomical Unit (AU) | The mean Earth–Sun distance, about 150 million km |

### Life cycle of stars
| Star mass | Path |
|---|---|
| Sun-like (low/medium) | Nebula → protostar → **main sequence** → **red giant** → planetary nebula → **white dwarf** (→ black dwarf, in theory) |
| Massive (> about 8 solar masses) | Nebula → main sequence → **red supergiant** → **supernova** → **neutron star** or **black hole** |
| Chandrasekhar limit | about **1.4 solar masses**, the maximum mass for a white dwarf |
| Energy source | Nuclear **fusion** of hydrogen into helium in the core |

### The planets
| Planet | Remember for |
|---|---|
| Mercury | Smallest; nearest to the Sun; shortest year (88 days); no moons |
| Venus | **Hottest**; brightest planet ("morning/evening star"); **retrograde** rotation; Earth's "twin" in size; no moons |
| Earth | Only known life; the "Blue Planet"; densest planet |
| Mars | "Red Planet" (iron oxide); Olympus Mons; moons Phobos and Deimos |
| Jupiter | **Largest**; Great Red Spot; fastest rotation (about 10 h); Galilean moons Io, Europa, Ganymede, Callisto |
| Saturn | Most prominent rings; **least dense** (would float); Titan |
| Uranus | Tilt about 98° ("rolls" on its side); **retrograde** rotation; Herschel, 1781 |
| Neptune | Farthest planet; discovered **by mathematics**, 1846 (Galle; Le Verrier & Adams); strongest winds |

Inner (terrestrial, rocky): Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars. Outer (Jovian): Jupiter and Saturn (gas giants), Uranus and Neptune (ice giants).

## 2. Memory hooks

1. **Planet order: "My Very Educated Mother Just Served Us Nachos."** Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune. (Pluto's "Pizza" was taken off the menu in 2006.)
2. **Dwarf planets: "Can Pluto Have Many Eggs?"** **C**eres, **P**luto, **H**aumea, **M**akemake, **E**ris. This is also their rough distance order from the Sun.
3. **Two ages, two numbers: "13.8 is the Universe, 4.6 is home".** Think of the universe as a 13.8-year-old teenager and the solar system as a 4.6-year-old child born in it. Do not confuse the parent's age with the child's.
4. **Venus is the backwards greenhouse.** Venus is a car parked in summer sun with the windows shut: the hottest, even though Mercury is closer. It also "drives in reverse", rotating from east to west. **V**enus and **U**ranus are the two "rebels": the two retrograde rotators.
5. **Uranus on its side.** Uranus is a **bowling ball rolling down the lane**. Its tilt of about 98° makes it roll rather than spin like a top.
6. **Neptune = the planet found with a pen.** Le Verrier and Adams "found" it on paper first, and Galle then pointed the telescope (1846). "**N**eptune = **N**umbers."
7. **Star life as a human life.** Main sequence is adulthood (the longest stage, burning hydrogen). Red giant is old age, when the star swells up. A white dwarf is the retired star: a small, hot ember. A heavy star "dies dramatically" (supernova) and leaves a neutron star or black hole. **The Chandrasekhar limit of 1.4 is the "weight limit for a peaceful retirement".**
8. **Origin hypotheses: "One star → Two stars" in date order.** **K**ant → **L**aplace (one-star, 1700s) → **C**hamberlin–**M**oulton → **J**eans–**J**effreys → **R**ussell (two-star, 1900s). Sentence: "**K**ind **L**adies **C**ook **M**angoes, **J**olly **J**ugglers **R**un." **Jeans–Jeffreys** are a pair of "**J**-twins" (tidal), and Chamberlin–Moulton is the "**CM**" pair (planetesimal).
9. **Perihelion in January (a counter-intuitive hook).** "The Earth hugs the Sun in **J**anuary and walks away in **J**uly." Both months start with J, so remember "**hug in Jan, leave in Jul**". Seasons come from the tilt, not the distance.
10. **Eclipse rule: "Whoever is in the middle blocks the light."** **S**olar: the **M**oon is in the middle, on a **N**ew moon ("SMN = **S**un **M**issing at **N**oon"). **L**unar: the **E**arth is in the middle, on a **F**ull moon ("LEF = **L**ight **E**arth **F**ull").
11. **Moons: "Ganymede > Mercury".** The largest moon is bigger than the smallest planet: "the moon that outgrew a planet". Titan is the moon in a blanket (thick atmosphere).
12. **CMB = the "afterglow photo".** The Big Bang's leftover heat is like the warmth left in a tandoor after the fire goes out. Penzias and Wilson first thought it was pigeon droppings in their antenna.

## 3. Don't mix up
| Pair | Difference |
|---|---|
| Hottest vs closest planet | Venus is the hottest; Mercury is the closest |
| Largest vs least dense planet | Jupiter is the largest; Saturn is the least dense |
| Ganymede vs Titan | Ganymede is the largest moon; Titan is second and has a thick atmosphere |
| Asteroid belt vs Kuiper Belt | Mars–Jupiter vs beyond Neptune |
| Solar vs lunar eclipse | New moon (Moon in middle) vs full moon (Earth in middle) |
| Sidereal vs solar day | About 23 h 56 min vs 24 h |
| Perihelion vs aphelion | About 3 January (near) vs about 4 July (far) |
| Nebular vs Planetesimal | Kant/Laplace (one star) vs Chamberlin–Moulton (two stars) |
| Tidal vs Binary | Jeans–Jeffreys vs Russell |
| Light-year vs year | A light-year measures distance, not time |
| White dwarf vs neutron star | Remnant of a Sun-like star vs remnant of a supernova of a massive star |

## 4. Exam traps
- "Hottest planet" is **Venus**, not Mercury.
- **Ceres** is a dwarf planet, while **Titan** and **Charon** are moons.
- Pluto was reclassified in **2006 at Prague** (IAU), and is no longer the "9th planet".
- Answer moon counts with "as per the latest data". Saturn and Jupiter keep gaining newly confirmed moons, so the exact count changes over time.
- A **light-year** is a distance, and a **parsec** (about 3.26 light-years) is also a distance.
- The Earth is **closest to the Sun in January**, which is winter in the Northern Hemisphere.
- In the **Chronology** of a massive star, the red **supergiant** comes **before** the supernova; the black hole or neutron star comes last.
