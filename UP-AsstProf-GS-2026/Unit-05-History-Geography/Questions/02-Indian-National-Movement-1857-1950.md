## Solutions
**Q1 — (D).** Lucknow: Begum Hazrat Mahal (for her son Birjis Qadr). Kanpur: Nana Saheb (with Tantia Tope). Bareilly: Khan Bahadur Khan. Faizabad: Maulvi Ahmadullah. *Also remember:* the revolt began at Meerut on 10 May 1857. Jhansi was led by Rani Lakshmibai, who was defeated by Hugh Rose, and Lucknow and Kanpur were recaptured by Sir Colin Campbell.

**Q2 — (A).** W.C. Bonnerjee presided over the first session (28 December 1885, Gokuldas Tejpal Sanskrit College, Bombay), which was attended by 72 delegates. A.O. Hume was the founder. *Also remember:* the 1888 Allahabad session was presided over by George Yule, the first non-Indian (English) president.

**Q3 — (B).** Partition of Bengal (16 October 1905) → Surat Split (1907) → Morley–Minto Reforms (1909) → Lucknow Pact (1916). *Also remember:* the partition was annulled in 1911 (Delhi Durbar), and the capital moved from Calcutta to Delhi in the same year.

**Q4 — (A).** The violence at Chauri Chaura (4–5 February 1922) led Gandhi to call off the movement, so R explains A. *Also remember:* the movement was launched in 1920, after the special Calcutta session (September 1920) and the Nagpur session (December 1920).

**Q5 — (C).** Statements 1 and 2 are correct. Ram Prasad Bismil, Ashfaqulla Khan, Rajendra Lahiri and Roshan Singh were hanged in the Kakori case. Chandrashekhar Azad evaded arrest and died at Alfred Park, Allahabad, in February 1931. *Also remember:* the HRA became the HSRA in 1928 (Delhi, Feroz Shah Kotla), with Bhagat Singh among its members. Kakori is in Lucknow district, and the event is now officially called the "Kakori Train Action".

**Q6 — (D).** Simon Commission arrives (February 1928) → Nehru Report (August 1928) → Lahore Purna Swaraj resolution (December 1929) → Dandi March begins (12 March 1930). *Also remember:* the Simon Commission was boycotted because it had no Indian member ("Simon Go Back"), and Lala Lajpat Rai died after a lathi-charge at Lahore.

**Q7 — (B).** Gandhi attended only the Second RTC (September–December 1931), following the Gandhi–Irwin Pact (March 1931). *Also remember:* B.R. Ambedkar attended all three RTCs, and the Congress boycotted the First and Third.

**Q8 — (D).** The 1925 Kanpur session was presided over by Sarojini Naidu, the first Indian woman president of the INC. Annie Besant (Calcutta, 1917) was the first woman president. *Also remember:* the Lucknow session of 1916 reunited the Moderates and Extremists and produced the Congress–League Pact.

**Q9 — (C).** Cripps Mission (March 1942) → Quit India Resolution (8 August 1942, Gowalia Tank, Bombay) → Azad Hind government proclaimed by Subhas Chandra Bose (21 October 1943, Singapore) → Cabinet Mission (March 1946). *Also remember:* the slogan "Do or Die" belongs to Quit India, and the INA trials took place at the Red Fort (1945–46).

**Q10 — (B).** Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha, the oldest member, was the provisional President. Dr. Rajendra Prasad was elected permanent President on 11 December 1946. *Also remember:* Nehru moved the Objectives Resolution on 13 December 1946. It was adopted on 22 January 1947 and later became the basis of the Preamble.

**Q11 — (A).** Statements 1 and 2 are correct. The Act abolished the office of the **Secretary of State for India**, while the office of Governor-General continued in each dominion (Mountbatten became India's first Governor-General). *Also remember:* the Act followed the Mountbatten Plan of 3 June 1947, and C. Rajagopalachari was the first and last Indian Governor-General.

**Q12 — (C).** Partition of Bengal: Curzon. Jallianwala Bagh (13 April 1919): Chelmsford. Simon Commission (1927–28): Irwin. Quit India (1942): Linlithgow. *Also remember:* Lord Canning was the Governor-General during the 1857 revolt and became the first Viceroy in 1858.
