## Solutions
**Q1 — (B).** The universe is about 13.8 billion years old. *Also remember:* the Sun and solar system are about 4.6 billion years old. That is distractor (A).

**Q2 — (A).** Nebular: Kant (1755), refined by Laplace (1796). Planetesimal: Chamberlin and Moulton (1904). Tidal: Jeans (1919) and Jeffreys. Binary star: H.N. Russell. *Also remember:* Otto Schmidt proposed the interstellar dust (accretion) hypothesis, and C.F. von Weizsäcker revised the nebular hypothesis. The modern view is the **solar nebular disc model**.

**Q3 — (C).** Statements 1 and 3 are correct. Venus has retrograde rotation (the Sun rises in the west there). **Venus**, not Mercury, is the hottest planet, because its thick CO₂ atmosphere causes a runaway greenhouse effect. *Also remember:* Uranus is the other planet with retrograde rotation.

**Q4 — (A).** Subrahmanyan Chandrasekhar showed that a white dwarf heavier than about 1.4 solar masses cannot be supported by electron degeneracy pressure and collapses further. He won the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1983. *Also remember:* NASA's Chandra X-ray Observatory is named after him.

**Q5 — (C).** The assertion is true. The reason is false: in a lunar eclipse the **Earth** comes between the Sun and the Moon. *Also remember:* a solar eclipse occurs on a new moon (amavasya), when the Moon is between the Sun and the Earth. Eclipses do not happen every month because the Moon's orbit is tilted by about 5° to the ecliptic.

**Q6 — (D).** Titan is Saturn's largest moon, not a dwarf planet. The five IAU dwarf planets are Ceres, Pluto, Haumea, Makemake and Eris. *Also remember:* Pluto was reclassified as a dwarf planet at the IAU General Assembly in Prague in 2006. Ceres is the only dwarf planet in the asteroid belt.

**Q7 — (B).** Nebula → protostar → main sequence → red supergiant → supernova → neutron star or black hole. *Also remember:* a Sun-like star instead goes main sequence → red giant → planetary nebula → white dwarf.

**Q8 — (D).** Ganymede (Jupiter) is the largest moon and is even larger than Mercury. *Also remember:* Titan (Saturn) is second and is the only moon with a thick atmosphere. The four Galilean moons of Jupiter are Io, Europa, Ganymede and Callisto.

**Q9 — (A).** Statements 1 and 2 are correct. Perihelion is around 3 January and aphelion around 4 July. A sidereal day (about 23 h 56 min) is **shorter** than a solar day (24 h). *Also remember:* the equinoxes fall around 21 March and 23 September, and the December solstice (about 22 December) puts the Sun over the Tropic of Capricorn.

**Q10 — (B).** Penzias and Wilson (Bell Labs) discovered the CMB in 1965 and won the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1978. *Also remember:* Lemaître first proposed the "primeval atom" idea, and Hubble (1929) showed that galaxies are receding (an expanding universe). Hoyle backed the rival Steady State theory and coined the name "Big Bang".

**Q11 — (D).** Mars: Olympus Mons. Saturn: least dense. Uranus: tilted about 98°. Neptune: predicted mathematically (Le Verrier, Adams) and found by Galle in 1846. *Also remember:* Uranus was discovered by William Herschel in 1781, the first planet discovered with a telescope.

**Q12 — (C).** The main asteroid belt lies between Mars and Jupiter. *Also remember:* the Kuiper Belt lies beyond Neptune (Pluto is in it), and the Oort Cloud is the distant source of long-period comets.
