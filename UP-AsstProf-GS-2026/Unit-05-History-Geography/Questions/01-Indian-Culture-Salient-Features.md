## Solutions
**Q1 — (B).** Alamgirpur (Meerut, on the Hindon, a Yamuna tributary) is the easternmost Harappan site. *Also remember:* Sinauli (Baghpat, UP) is famous for its Bronze Age burials with "chariots"; Kalibangan is in Rajasthan and Banawali in Haryana.

**Q2 — (C).** Lothal (Gujarat) has the dockyard, Kalibangan (Rajasthan) the ploughed field, Dholavira (Gujarat) the signboard with ten large signs, and Mohenjo-daro (Sindh) the Great Bath. *Also remember:* Dholavira became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2021. Harappa was excavated by Daya Ram Sahni (1921) and Mohenjo-daro by R.D. Banerji (1922).

**Q3 — (D).** All three are correct. The Rigveda has 10 mandalas and about 1,028 hymns. *Also remember:* "Satyameva Jayate" comes from the Mundaka Upanishad, not the Rigveda.

**Q4 — (A).** Sankhya–Kapila, Yoga–Patanjali, Nyaya–Gautama, Vaisheshika–Kanada. *Also remember:* the remaining two orthodox (astika) darshanas are Purva Mimamsa (Jaimini) and Uttara Mimamsa/Vedanta (Badarayana).

**Q5 — (B).** Both statements are true, but the date of adoption does not explain why the Sarnath capital was chosen. *Also remember:* the emblem's motto "Satyameva Jayate" is in Devanagari script, and the original capital has four lions, of which three are visible in the emblem.

**Q6 — (C).** Sarnath was inscribed on 25 July 2026 at the 48th session of the World Heritage Committee in Busan (South Korea). It is India's 45th World Heritage property (as of November 2026) and Uttar Pradesh's 4th. *Also remember:* UP's other three are the Taj Mahal and Agra Fort (both 1983) and Fatehpur Sikri (1986). The 44th was the Maratha Military Landscapes (2025).

**Q7 — (D).** The Konark Sun Temple (Odisha) is in the Kalinga style, a regional form of the Nagara (northern) style, not Dravida. *Also remember:* Vesara is a hybrid of Nagara and Dravida, typical of the Chalukya and Hoysala temples of Karnataka.

**Q8 — (A).** Kathak (from "katha", story) flourished at the court of Wajid Ali Shah in Lucknow. *Also remember:* the four Kathak gharanas are Lucknow, Jaipur, Banaras and Raigarh. Pandit Birju Maharaj belonged to the Lucknow gharana.

**Q9 — (B).** Sanchi stupa (Ashoka, 3rd century BCE) → Kailasa Temple, Ellora (Rashtrakuta Krishna I, 8th century) → Brihadeshwara (Rajaraja Chola I, completed c. 1010) → Taj Mahal (Shah Jahan, 17th century). *Also remember:* Brihadeshwara is part of the UNESCO site "Great Living Chola Temples".

**Q10 — (C).** The Kumbh Mela was inscribed in 2017. *Also remember:* Ramlila was inscribed in 2008, and Vedic chanting was also inscribed that year. Kumbh is held at Prayagraj, Haridwar, Ujjain and Nashik.

**Q11 — (A).** Statements 1 and 2 are correct. Amir Khusrau lived in the Delhi Sultanate period (he was a poet at the court of Alauddin Khalji and others) and was a disciple of Nizamuddin Auliya. He was not at Akbar's court. *Also remember:* Tansen was one of the "Navaratnas" of Akbar's court and is associated with Dhrupad.

**Q12 — (D).** The *Ramcharitmanas* is written in Awadhi, the language of the Ayodhya–Lucknow region. *Also remember:* Surdas wrote the *Sursagar* in Braj Bhasha, and Malik Muhammad Jayasi wrote the *Padmavat* in Awadhi.
