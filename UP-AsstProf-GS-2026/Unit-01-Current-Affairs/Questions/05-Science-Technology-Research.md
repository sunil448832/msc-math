## Solutions
**Q1 — (B).** IceCube is a cubic kilometre of Antarctic ice at the **South Pole**, fitted with about 5,160 light sensors. Halzen (University of Wisconsin–Madison, born in Belgium) was the **sole** laureate. The prize was announced on 6 October 2026 (October 2026). *Also remember:* he is the first sole Physics laureate since Pierre-Gilles de Gennes (1991). India's proposed **INO** (India-based Neutrino Observatory) is at Bodi West Hills, Theni, Tamil Nadu. Kamioka (Japan) is the site of Super-Kamiokande.

**Q2 — (C).** Medicine 2026 (5 October 2026): Karl Deisseroth, Peter Hegemann and Georg Nagel, for light-gated ion channels and optogenetics. Chemistry 2026 (7 October 2026): Henri B. Kagan (France) and Kenso Soai (Japan), "for the discovery of non-linear effects and autocatalysis in asymmetric organic synthesis". Physics 2026: Halzen, for neutrinos. Chemistry 2025: Susumu Kitagawa, Richard Robson and Omar Yaghi, for MOFs (October 2025). *Also remember:* the 2026 Peace Prize went to Navi Pillay (South Africa) and Literature to Anne Carson (Canada). Each prize is worth 12 million SEK, shared between the laureates.

**Q3 — (A).** The 2025 Medicine Nobel was for **peripheral immune tolerance**: regulatory T cells (Tregs) and the FOXP3 gene, which stop the immune system from attacking the body's own tissues (October 2025). *Also remember:* microRNA won in 2024 (Ambros, Ruvkun), mRNA vaccines in 2023 (Karikó, Weissman), and Hepatitis C in 2020.

**Q4 — (D).** All three statements are correct. Clarke, Devoret and Martinis showed quantum tunnelling and discrete energy levels in a circuit large enough to hold in the hand, built from superconductors separated by a thin insulator (a Josephson junction) (October 2025). *Also remember:* this work underlies **superconducting qubits** in quantum computers. India's **National Quantum Mission** was approved in 2023.

**Q5 — (B).** Group Captain **Shubhanshu Shukla** (IAF), born in **Lucknow**, flew on Axiom-4 in June–July 2025 and became the first Indian on the ISS. He is the second Indian in space, after Rakesh Sharma in 1984. He received the **Ashoka Chakra**, India's highest peacetime gallantry award, on Republic Day 2026 (January 2026). *Also remember:* the other three are Gaganyaan astronaut-designates: P.B. Nair (Ax-4 backup), Ajit Krishnan and Angad Pratap.

**Q6 — (C).** A is true. G1 carries **Vyommitra**, a half-humanoid robot, on an uncrewed orbital test flight. As of October 2026 ISRO was still aiming to fly it by the end of 2026, after the March 2026 target slipped. R is false: Gaganyaan uses the **human-rated LVM3 (HLVM3)**, not the PSLV. *Also remember:* the first crewed flight is targeted for 2027 or later. India plans the **Bharatiya Antariksh Station** by 2035.

**Q7 — (D).** BlueBird Block-2 belongs to **AST SpaceMobile** (USA). It is a satellite that connects directly to ordinary smartphones. The launch on 24 December 2025 IST was a commercial mission through NSIL (December 2025). *Also remember:* LVM3-M5/**CMS-03** (2 November 2025, about 4,400 kg) was the heaviest communication satellite launched from India to GTO. The two LVM3 launches had the shortest gap ever between them.

**Q8 — (B).** NISAR (30 July 2025) → CMS-03 (2 November 2025) → PSLV-C62 (12 January 2026) → Artemis II (1 April 2026). *Also remember:* NISAR is the first NASA–ISRO joint Earth-observation satellite. It uses dual-frequency SAR (L-band from NASA, S-band from ISRO).

**Q9 — (A).** PSLV-C62 carried DRDO's **EOS-N1 "Anvesha"** and about 14 co-passenger payloads. A deviation in the third stage meant they did not reach orbit (January 2026). It was PSLV's **second failure in a row**, after PSLV-C61 (EOS-09) in May 2025. *Also remember:* PSLV's first developmental flight also failed, in 1993. A national expert committee reviewed the C62 failure.

**Q10 — (C).** Statements 1 and 2 are correct. Artemis II (launched 1 April 2026, splashed down off San Diego on 10 April 2026) flew Reid Wiseman, Victor Glover, Christina Koch and Jeremy Hansen (CSA) around the Moon. It set a new record for the farthest humans have travelled from Earth (April 2026). Statement 3 is wrong: Artemis II was a **flyby with no landing**. A landing is planned for a later Artemis mission. *Also remember:* India signed the **Artemis Accords** in June 2023.

**Q11 — (D).** Neutrinos are **nearly massless and electrically neutral**, and they interact only weakly with matter. That is why detectors need huge volumes such as IceCube's ice. All the other descriptions are correct. *Also remember:* a **ramjet** burns fuel in subsonic airflow, a **scramjet** in supersonic airflow. Both are air-breathing and take oxygen from the atmosphere. Rockets carry their own oxidiser.

**Q12 — (A).** The tests at DRDL's Scramjet Connect Pipe Test facility support the **Hypersonic Cruise Missile Development Programme**. The January 2026 run lasted about 12 minutes and the May 2026 run more than 1,200 seconds (January and May 2026). *Also remember:* "hypersonic" means faster than **Mach 5**. India's earlier milestones include HSTDV (2020) and the long-range hypersonic missile flight test from Dr APJ Abdul Kalam Island in November 2024.
