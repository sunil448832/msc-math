# Cheatsheet · Unit 1 · Topic 05 — Fields, Finite Fields, Field Extensions
> Questions: [../Questions/05-Fields-Finite-Fields-Extensions.md](../Questions/05-Fields-Finite-Fields-Extensions.md)

## 1. Basics
- The **characteristic** of a field is $0$ or a prime $p$. The **prime subfield** is $\mathbb{Q}$ in characteristic 0 and $\mathbb{F}_p$ in characteristic $p$.
- In characteristic $p$: $(a+b)^p=a^p+b^p$ (the Frobenius map is a homomorphism, and it is injective).
- A ring homomorphism out of a field is either injective or zero.

## 2. Extensions and degree
- $[K:F]=\dim_F K$. **Tower law:** $[L:F]=[L:K][K:F]$ for $F\subseteq K\subseteq L$. Hence the degree of any intermediate field divides $[L:F]$.
- $\alpha$ is **algebraic** over $F$ if it is a root of a non-zero $f\in F[x]$. Its **minimal polynomial** $m_\alpha$ is the monic irreducible polynomial with root $\alpha$, and it divides every $f$ with $f(\alpha)=0$.
- $F(\alpha)\cong F[x]/\langle m_\alpha\rangle$ and $[F(\alpha):F]=\deg m_\alpha$, with basis $1,\alpha,\dots,\alpha^{n-1}$.
- **$\alpha$ transcendental** gives $F(\alpha)\cong F(x)$ (rational functions), of infinite degree. $\pi$ and $e$ are transcendental.
- **Finite ⟹ algebraic**, but not conversely ($\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}$ is algebraic of infinite degree). Sums, products and inverses of algebraic elements are algebraic. Algebraic over algebraic is algebraic.
- $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ is countable, so **almost all real numbers are transcendental**. $[\mathbb{R}:\mathbb{Q}]=\infty$, and $\mathbb{C}$ is algebraically closed with $[\mathbb{C}:\mathbb{R}]=2$.
- If $\gcd([F(\alpha):F],[F(\beta):F])=1$ then $[F(\alpha,\beta):F]$ equals the product of the two degrees.
- If $[F(\alpha):F]$ is odd then $F(\alpha)=F(\alpha^2)$.

## 3. Splitting fields
- The **splitting field** of $f\in F[x]$ is the smallest extension in which $f$ splits into linear factors. It exists and is unique up to isomorphism.
- If $\deg f=n$, then $[K:F]\le n!$, and $[K:F]$ divides $n!$.
- **Normal extension** = the splitting field of some polynomial. **Separable**: every minimal polynomial has distinct roots, which is automatic in characteristic 0 and over finite fields (perfect fields).
- $f$ has a repeated root $\iff\gcd(f,f')\ne1$.
- Primitive element theorem: a finite separable extension is $F(\alpha)$ for a single $\alpha$.
- **Galois:** for a finite normal separable extension, $|\operatorname{Gal}(K/F)|=[K:F]$.

## 4. Finite fields
- $|F|=p^n$, where $p=\operatorname{char}F$ and $n=[F:\mathbb{F}_p]$. **For each prime power $q=p^n$ there is exactly one field up to isomorphism**, $GF(q)=\mathbb{F}_q$, namely the splitting field of $x^q-x$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$, which consists exactly of the roots of $x^q-x$.
- No field of order $6,10,12,\dots$ (non-prime-powers).
- $\mathbb{F}_q^{\times}$ is **cyclic** of order $q-1$, so there are $\varphi(q-1)$ primitive elements. Every element satisfies $a^q=a$.
- **Subfields:** $\mathbb{F}_{p^m}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p^n}\iff m\mid n$. The number of subfields is $d(n)$, and $\mathbb{F}_{p^m}$ and $\mathbb{F}_{p^k}$ together generate $\mathbb{F}_{p^{\operatorname{lcm}(m,k)}}$.
- $\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{F}_{p^n}/\mathbb{F}_p)=\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{F}_{p^n})\cong\mathbb{Z}_n$, generated by Frobenius $x\mapsto x^p$.
- **Construction:** $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}\cong\mathbb{F}_p[x]/\langle f\rangle$ for any irreducible $f$ of degree $n$. E.g. $\mathbb{F}_4=\mathbb{F}_2[x]/\langle x^2+x+1\rangle$, $\mathbb{F}_8=\mathbb{F}_2[x]/\langle x^3+x+1\rangle$, $\mathbb{F}_9=\mathbb{F}_3[x]/\langle x^2+1\rangle$.
- $x^{p^n}-x=\prod$ (all monic irreducibles over $\mathbb{F}_p$ of degree $d\mid n$). Comparing degrees gives $p^n=\sum_{d\mid n}d\,N_p(d)$.
- **Number of monic irreducibles of degree $n$ over $\mathbb{F}_q$:**
$$N_q(n)=\frac1n\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(d)\,q^{n/d}$$

Over $\mathbb{F}_2$: the degree 2 irreducible is $x^2+x+1$. Degree 3: $x^3+x+1$, $x^3+x^2+1$. Degree 4: $x^4+x+1$, $x^4+x^3+1$, $x^4+x^3+x^2+x+1$.
- Every element of a finite field is a sum of two squares. In $\mathbb{F}_q$ with $q$ odd, exactly $(q-1)/2$ non-zero elements are squares.
- $\mathbb{F}_q[x]/\langle f\rangle$ has $q^{\deg f}$ elements, and it is a field iff $f$ is irreducible.

## 5. Constructibility (straightedge and compass)
- $\alpha$ is constructible iff there is a tower $\mathbb{Q}=K_0\subset\dots\subset K_r\ni\alpha$ with each step of degree 2. **Necessary condition:** $[\mathbb{Q}(\alpha):\mathbb{Q}]=2^k$. (The degree is not sufficient on its own.)
- Impossible: **doubling the cube** ($\sqrt[3]2$ has degree 3); **trisecting $60^\circ$** ($\cos20^\circ$ is a root of the irreducible $8x^3-6x-1$); **squaring the circle** ($\pi$ is transcendental).
- **Regular $n$-gon (Gauss–Wantzel):** constructible iff $n=2^k p_1\cdots p_r$ with the $p_i$ distinct Fermat primes ($3,5,17,257,65537$), iff $\varphi(n)$ is a power of 2. Constructible: 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 12, 15, 16, 17. Not constructible: 7, 9, 11, 13, 14, 18.
- Constructible angles include $30^\circ,45^\circ,72^\circ,3^\circ$. Not constructible: $20^\circ,40^\circ,10^\circ,1^\circ$.

## Exam traps
- **Check containment before multiplying degrees:** $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt2,\sqrt8)=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt2)$ and $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt2,\sqrt{18})=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt2)$, but $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt2,\sqrt3)$ has degree 4.
- The splitting field of $x^4+1$ has degree 4, not 8. The splitting field of $x^4-2$ has degree 8.
- The splitting field of $x^3-2$ has degree 6, not 3: $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]2)$ is not normal.
- $\mathbb{F}_4\not\subseteq\mathbb{F}_8$ since $2\nmid3$. And $\mathbb{F}_4\ne\mathbb{Z}_4$: $\mathbb{Z}_4$ is not a field.
- $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}^{\times}$ has $\varphi(p^n-1)$ generators, not $\varphi(p^n)$.
- Algebraic does not imply finite. Finite does imply algebraic.
- Degree $2^k$ is necessary but not sufficient for constructibility (some degree-4 numbers with Galois group $S_4$ are not constructible).
