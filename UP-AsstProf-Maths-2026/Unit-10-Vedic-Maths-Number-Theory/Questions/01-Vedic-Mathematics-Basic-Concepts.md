## Solutions
**Q1 — (B).** Swami Bharati Krishna Tirtha (1884–1960), Shankaracharya of Govardhana Math, Puri, wrote *Vedic Mathematics*. The other three are ancient or medieval mathematicians.

**Q2 — (C).** The deficiencies from 100 are 3 and 4. Left part: $97-4 = 96-3 = 93$. Right part: $3\times4 = 12$, written as two digits. Answer $9312$. (A) comes from subtracting wrongly.

**Q3 — (A).** Ekadhikena = "by one more than the previous one"; Nikhilam = "all from 9 and the last from 10"; Urdhva-Tiryagbhyam = "vertically and crosswise"; Paravartya Yojayet = "transpose and apply".

**Q4 — (C).** $1/19$: the denominator ends in 9, and the digit before it is 1, so the ekadhika is $1+1=2$. Start with 1 at the right and keep multiplying by 2 (with carries) from right to left: $1/19=0.\overline{052631578947368421}$. Since $\operatorname{ord}_{19}10=18$, the period is 18. (B) halves the period wrongly. (D) mixes up the ekadhika 2 with the auxiliary denominator $19+1=20$.

**Q5 — (B).** I is true. II is true: the book was published posthumously in 1965 (the author died in 1960). III is false: the author said the sutras came from a *parishishta* (appendix) of the Atharvaveda, but they are not found in the standard extant Atharvaveda text. This is the usual criticism of the claim.

**Q6 — (A).** The leading parts are both 4 and the last digits $7+3=10$, so the result is $4\times(4+1)=20$ followed by $7\times3=21$, giving $2021$. R states exactly that rule, so it explains A.

**Q7 — (B).** The deficiency from 1000 is 4. Left part: $996-4=992$. Right part: $4^2=16$, padded to three digits (the base has 3 zeros) as $016$. Answer $992016$. (A) forgets the padding.

**Q8 — (D).** Digit roots: $12345\to 6$ and $6789\to 30\to 3$, so the product has digit root $6\times3=18\to 9$. Only $83810205$ (digit sum 27, root 9) passes. The others have digit roots 1, 1 and 8. (The check can only rule answers out; here only one option survives it.)

**Q9 — (D).** $4\bar{3}=40-3=37$, not 43. The others are correct: $20-1=19$, $300-11=289$ and $80-4=76$.

**Q10 — (B).** Urdhva-Tiryagbhyam ("vertically and crosswise") is the general method. In modern terms it is the convolution of the digit sequences. Nikhilam works best near a base, Ekanyunena only for multipliers made of 9s, and Shunyam Samyasamuccaye is for solving equations.

**Q11 — (C).** With the flag digits $\bar1\bar2$: the dividend splits as $12\,|\,34$. Bring down 1. Then $2+(1)(\bar1)=1$. In the remainder columns: $3+(1)(\bar2)+(1)(\bar1)=0$ and $4+(1)(\bar2)=2$. Quotient $11$, remainder $02$. Check: $112\times11+2=1234$. (D) is the result of an unreduced negative remainder.

**Q12 — (D).** $N_1+N_2=4x+5$ and $D_1+D_2=8x+10=2(4x+5)$. The sutra (when the sums are the same, apart from a numerical factor) gives $4x+5=0$, so $x=-5/4$. Cross-multiplying confirms it: $6x^2+17x+12=6x^2+13x+7\Rightarrow x=-5/4$.

**Q13 — (A).** Left part: $457-1=456$. Right part: the complement of 456 from 999, which is $543$. So $457\times999=456543$ (indeed $457000-457$).
