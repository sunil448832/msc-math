## Solutions
**Q1 — (C).** *Lilavati* is the arithmetic part of Bhaskara II's *Siddhanta Shiromani* (c. 1150). The other parts are *Bijaganita*, *Grahaganita* and *Goladhyaya*. Do not confuse Bhaskara II with Bhaskara I (7th century).

**Q2 — (B).** Aryabhatiya (499 CE) is by Aryabhata I, Brahmasphutasiddhanta (628) by Brahmagupta, Ganitasarasangraha (c. 850) by Mahavira, and Tantrasangraha (c. 1500) by Nilakantha Somayaji.

**Q3 — (C).** $s = (7+15+20+24)/2 = 33$, so the area is $\sqrt{(33-7)(33-15)(33-20)(33-24)} = \sqrt{26\cdot18\cdot13\cdot9}=\sqrt{54756}=234$. Check: $7^2+24^2=15^2+20^2=25^2$, so the quadrilateral is two right triangles on a diameter of 25, with area $84+150=234$.

**Q4 — (A).** $62832/20000 = 3.1416$. $22/7$ is a later popular value, and $\sqrt{10}$ was used by Brahmagupta and others as a rough value.

**Q5 — (D).** All three are standard facts. Kuttaka ("pulveriser") goes back to Aryabhata I. Bhavana is Brahmagupta's composition law (Brahmagupta's identity). Chakravala is expounded in Bhaskara II's *Bijaganita*, with the well-known example $x^2-61y^2=1$.

**Q6 — (A).** Madhava (c. 1340–1425) found $\arctan x = x - x^3/3 + x^5/5-\cdots$, and $x=1$ gives the series for $\pi/4$. Both statements are true and R is the derivation of A. This is why the series is now often called the Madhava–Leibniz series.

**Q7 — (D).** Lilavati is by Bhaskara II. Mahavira wrote the *Ganitasarasangraha*. The other three pairs are correct.

**Q8 — (C).** $1+\tfrac13+\tfrac1{12}-\tfrac1{408} = \tfrac{408+136+34-1}{408}=\tfrac{577}{408}\approx1.4142157$. The other options are also good approximations of $\sqrt2$ (Pell convergents), so you have to compute.

**Q9 — (B).** $1151^2 = 1324801 = 92\cdot 14400+1$ ✓. Brahmagupta got it by starting from $10^2-92\cdot1^2=8$ and applying bhavana. (A) gives $99^2-9200=601$, (C) gives $-22171$ and (D) gives $22001$, none of which is 1.

**Q10 — (A).** Madhava of Sangamagrama (c. 1340–1425) is regarded as the founder. Nilakantha (Tantrasangraha) and Jyeshthadeva (Yuktibhasha) came later in the same school.

**Q11 — (B).** $x(\pi - x) = \tfrac{5\pi^2}{36}$. The numerator is $\tfrac{80\pi^2}{36}$ and the denominator is $5\pi^2 - \tfrac{20\pi^2}{36} = \tfrac{160\pi^2}{36}$, so the value is exactly $1/2$. The approximation is exact at $0$, $\pi/6$, $\pi/2$, $5\pi/6$ and $\pi$.

**Q12 — (D).** Aryabhata I (born 476 CE), Brahmagupta (born 598), Mahavira (c. 9th century), Bhaskara II (born 1114), Madhava (14th century).

**Q13 — (A).** $13 = 8+5 = 9+4$ ✓. By the CRT the solutions are $13 \pmod{72}$, so $85$ also works but is not the least.
