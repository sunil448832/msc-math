# Difficulty Review: HPSC Screening Test 2025 vs our mocks
*Based on the HPSC Assistant Professor Screening Test 2025 (Mathematics), 100 questions, in a coaching institute's copy. This is a different state's exam, so it is a guide to difficulty, not to the UP pattern. Use [00-Paper-Blueprint.md](00-Paper-Blueprint.md) for the UP pattern.*

## The short version
Mocks 01–03 felt easy mainly because of **question format**, not just topic level.

| | UPHESC paper / Mocks 01–03 | HPSC 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Direct one-answer questions | About 50 of the 70 maths questions | Almost none |
| "How many of these statements or pairs are correct?" | 0 in Mocks 01–03 | **60 of 100** (46 statements, 14 pairs) |
| Two-statement judgement (I / II) | About 5 (as Assertion–Reason) | **39 of 100** |
| Can you eliminate options? | Usually | Rarely: you must judge every statement |
| Typical level | M.Sc. textbook results | CSIR-NET Part B/C, with some TIFR-style traps |

A "how many are correct" question with three statements is effectively three questions. One wrong judgement loses the whole mark.

## What makes the HPSC paper hard
1. **Every statement must be judged.** There is no shortcut by spotting one wrong option.
2. **Near-identical statements.** The same object appears with one changed word: "$xe^{-nx^2}$ converges uniformly on $\mathbb{R}$" vs "only on compact subsets".
3. **Theorem hypotheses are tested, not formulas.** Examples: the Gauss–Lucas theorem, the Baire category theorem, Volterra vs Fredholm uniqueness, Picard–Lindelöf failing or blow-up.
4. **Non-standard objects.** Examples: $\mathbb{Z}[(1+\sqrt{-23})/2]$, a Sylow subgroup of $GL_{33}(\mathbb{F}_{11})$, and Rouché counting on an annulus.
5. **Disguised answers.** An option may be written as a series (such as $\sum(-1)^{n-1}/n^2$), so you must also evaluate the series.
6. **Several ideas in one question.** For example, a Riemann-sum limit fixes $k$, which then feeds a diagonalisability statement.

Two differences from the UP syllabus affect how far to copy this paper:
- HPSC has **probability and statistics**, which is **not** on the UP syllabus, so Mocks 04–05 leave it out.
- UP gives far more weight to **mechanics and fluids, OR, differential geometry and number theory/Vedic maths**, so Mocks 04–05 keep the UP unit counts.

**A realistic expectation:** these papers are deliberately harder than the last UP paper, so a low score here does not predict a low score in the exam. They are also tight for time: you are unlikely to finish every question, which is useful practice in choosing what to skip.

The questions are newly written. None are copied from the HPSC paper.
