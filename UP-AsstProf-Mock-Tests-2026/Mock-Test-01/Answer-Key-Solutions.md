**3 — (D)** LVM3-M6 lifted off from Sriharikota on 24 December 2025 and placed the roughly 6,100 kg BlueBird Block-2 of US firm AST SpaceMobile (direct-to-smartphone broadband) in LEO. It was a commercial mission through NSIL, and the payload beat the previous LVM3 record set by the OneWeb batch of about 5,700 kg.

**4 — (C)** On 11 December 2025, TIME named "The Architects of AI" (Altman, Huang, Zuckerberg, Musk and others) as its 2025 Person of the Year. This is a group or non-individual choice, like "You" in 2006. Donald Trump was the 2024 pick.

### Teaching & Research Aptitude (Q7–11)

**7 — (D)** Michael Scriven (1967, *The Methodology of Evaluation*) separated formative evaluation (during instruction, to improve it) from summative evaluation (at the end, to judge it). Glaser is linked with criterion-referenced testing, and Tyler with the objectives model.

**8 — (A)** Revised taxonomy: Remember → Understand → Apply → Analyse → Evaluate → **Create**, so the order is II, IV, I, III. Option (D) is the trap from the *original* 1956 Bloom, where Evaluation sat above Synthesis.

**9 — (B)** In snowball (chain-referral) sampling, existing subjects recruit further subjects, so selection probabilities are unknown and it is non-probability. Stratified, cluster and systematic sampling are all probability methods.

**10 — (C)** Action research is on-the-spot problem solving by practitioners. Kurt Lewin coined the term, and Stephen M. Corey brought it into education.

**11 — (D)** The Radhakrishnan Commission recommended the UGC in 1948–49. Maulana Azad inaugurated it on 28 December 1953, and it became a statutory body under the UGC Act, 1956.

### ICT (Q12–15)

**12 — (A)** $(2F)_{16} = 2\times16 + 15 = 47$ (checked in Python). The distractors $31=(1F)_{16}$ and $63=(3F)_{16}$ come from misreading the first digit.

**13 — (C)** Bcc means Blind Carbon Copy. Bcc addresses are hidden from all other recipients, while To and Cc addresses are visible to everyone.

**14 — (B)** SMTP is *Simple Mail **Transfer** Protocol*, used to send e-mail. POP3 and IMAP are used to receive it. The other three expansions are correct.

**15 — (D)** Phishing is a social-engineering fraud that uses fake e-mails or websites. Spamming means unsolicited bulk mail, a firewall is a security barrier, and cookies are small files that a website stores in the browser.

### People & Environment (Q16–19)

**16 — (D)** A is false: per molecule (and per kg) methane traps far more heat than CO₂, with a 100-year GWP of about 28. R is true, since rice paddies and livestock are major anthropogenic sources of methane.

**17 — (A)** The Minamata Convention on Mercury was adopted in 2013 and entered into force in 2017. It is named after the mercury poisoning disaster at Minamata, Japan.

**18 — (C)** Chipko began in 1973 in Mandal and Reni villages of Chamoli (then UP, now Uttarakhand). It is associated with Chandi Prasad Bhatt, Gaura Devi and Sunderlal Bahuguna.

**19 — (B)** The Sendai Framework was adopted at the 3rd UN World Conference on DRR in Sendai, Japan (March 2015) and covers 2015–2030. It succeeded the Hyogo Framework (2005–2015).

### History & Geography (Q20–23)

**20 — (D)** Jawaharlal Nehru presided at Lahore in December 1929. The tricolour was hoisted on the Ravi on 31 December, and 26 January 1930 was celebrated as Purna Swaraj Day. Motilal Nehru presided at Calcutta in 1928, Patel at Karachi in 1931, and Bose at Haripura in 1938.

**21 — (A)** Sankardeva created Sattriya in the 15th century in the *sattras* (monasteries) of Assam. The Sangeet Natak Akademi recognised it as a classical dance in 2000.

**22 — (A)** Venus's surface is about 465 °C, hotter than Mercury's despite its greater distance from the Sun. The cause is its thick CO₂ atmosphere (surface pressure about 92 bar) and the runaway greenhouse effect, so R correctly explains A.

**23 — (C)** The Godavari, about 1,465 km long, rises at Trimbakeshwar (Nashik) and is the largest peninsular river. Its size earns it the name 'Dakshin Ganga'.

### Constitution & Economy (Q24–30)

**24 — (B)** The 42nd Amendment, 1976 ("mini-Constitution") added 'Socialist', 'Secular' and 'Integrity'. This is the only amendment to the Preamble so far.

**25 — (C)** Art. 315 is the Public Service Commissions, Art. 324 the Election Commission, Art. 112 the annual financial statement (Budget) and Art. 40 village panchayats (DPSP).

**26 — (D)** Under Art. 118(4), the Speaker of Lok Sabha presides over a joint sitting. In the Speaker's absence the Deputy Speaker presides, and then the Deputy Chairman of Rajya Sabha. The Vice-President (Chairman of RS) never presides over a joint sitting.

**27 — (A)** The order is Planning Commission (15 March 1950) → Plan Holiday (1966–69) → Rolling Plan of the Janata Government (1978–80) → NITI Aayog (1 January 2015).

**28 — (B)** The Tendulkar Expert Group revised the poverty-line methodology and estimated poverty at 37.2% for 2004–05. The Rangarajan Committee (2014) later re-estimated it.

**29 — (A)** Qualitative (selective) tools include moral suasion, margin requirements, credit rationing and direct action. CRR, repo rate and OMOs are quantitative (general) tools.

**30 — (B)** Budget 2025–26 raised the Sec. 87A rebate so that income up to ₹12 lakh is tax-free under the new regime (₹12.75 lakh for salaried persons with the ₹75,000 standard deduction). ₹7 lakh was the earlier limit.

**31 — (B)** Generators of $C_n$ number $\varphi(n)$; $\varphi(12)=12(1-\tfrac12)(1-\tfrac13)=4$ (namely $a,a^5,a^7,a^{11}$).

**32 — (C)** Sylow: $n_5\equiv1\pmod5$ and $n_5\mid4$, so $n_5=1$ (the Sylow 5-subgroup is normal).

**33 — (A)** $15=3\cdot5$ and $3\nmid(5-1)$, so the standard $pq$-theorem (Sylow subgroups both normal, $G\cong C_3\times C_5\cong C_{15}$) gives (A); (R) is exactly the reason.

**34 — (D)** Order of a product of disjoint cycles $=\operatorname{lcm}$ of lengths $=\operatorname{lcm}(3,4,2)=12$.

**35 — (A)** $\mathbb Z[x]/\langle x\rangle\cong\mathbb Z$, an integral domain but not a field ⟹ prime, not maximal (e.g. $\langle x\rangle\subsetneq\langle 2,x\rangle$).

**36 — (C)** $\langle 2,x\rangle$ is not principal in $\mathbb Z[x]$, so $\mathbb Z[x]$ is a UFD but not a PID. (A), (B), (D) are standard true results.

**37 — (D)** $[\mathbb Q(\sqrt2):\mathbb Q]=2$, $[\mathbb Q(\sqrt[3]2):\mathbb Q]=3$; both divide the degree, which is $\le 6$, so it is $\operatorname{lcm}(2,3)=6$.

**38 — (C)** Symmetric $n\times n$ matrices have dimension $n(n+1)/2=6$ for $n=3$.

**39 — (A)** Rank–nullity: nullity $=\dim\mathbb R^4-\text{rank}=4-2=2$.

**40 — (B)** Hermitian → real; skew-Hermitian → purely imaginary or 0; unitary → $|\lambda|=1$; idempotent ($A^2=A$) → $\lambda^2=\lambda$, so 0 or 1.

**41 — (C)** The block $\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\0&2\end{pmatrix}$ is a Jordan block of size 2, so $(x-2)$ must appear squared; $(A-2I)(A-3I)\ne0$ but $(A-2I)^2(A-3I)=0$.

**42 — (D)** $\det(\operatorname{adj}A)=(\det A)^{n-1}=(1\cdot2\cdot3)^2=36$.

**43 — (B)** $R_2=2R_1$; $R_1,R_3$ are independent, so rank $=2$.

**44 — (A)** $\det\begin{pmatrix}1&2&k\\2&1&1\\3&3&2\end{pmatrix}=-1-2+3k=0\Rightarrow k=1$ (indeed $v_1+v_2=v_3$).

**45 — (D)** $\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\0&2\end{pmatrix}$ has the repeated eigenvalue 2 with only a 1-dimensional eigenspace (a Jordan block). (B) has distinct eigenvalues 1, 2; (C) is symmetric.

**46 — (C)** Radius of convergence 1. At $x=1$: harmonic series diverges; at $x=-1$: alternating series converges (Leibniz). Interval $[-1,1)$.

**47 — (B)** $C^1[a,b]\subset$ Lipschitz (bounded derivative, MVT) $\subset$ continuous $\subset$ Riemann integrable: III, IV, II, I.

**48 — (D)** $f(x,0)=f(0,y)=0$ ⟹ $f_x(0,0)=f_y(0,0)=0$ exist; along $y=mx$, $f\to m/(1+m^2)$, which depends on $m$, so $f$ is not continuous (hence not differentiable).

**49 — (D)** The pointwise limit of $x^n$ is 0 on $[0,1)$ and 1 at $x=1$, which is discontinuous; by (R) (true) the convergence cannot be uniform. So (A) is false.

**50 — (B)** $\mathbb Q\cap[0,1]$ is countable, so it has measure 0; the irrationals have measure $1-0=1$.

**51 — (A)** Standard theorem: a metric space is compact ⟺ it is complete and totally bounded. "Closed and bounded" suffices only in $\mathbb R^n$ (Heine–Borel).

**52 — (C)** $\ell^2$ and $L^2[0,1]$ are complete inner-product spaces. The sup-norm on $\ell^\infty$ and on $C[0,1]$ fails the parallelogram law, so these are Banach spaces but not Hilbert spaces.

**53 — (B)** This is Bessel's inequality; equality for every $x$ (a complete orthonormal set) is Parseval's identity.

**54 — (A)** For a pole of order 3, $\operatorname{Res}=\frac{1}{2!}\frac{d^2}{dz^2}e^z\big|_{z=1}=e/2$.

**55 — (C)** Both poles $\pm i$ lie inside $|z|=2$, with residues $\frac1{2i}$ and $-\frac1{2i}$; their sum is 0, so the integral is 0.

**56 — (D)** For $\operatorname{Im}z>0$, $z$ is closer to $i$ than to $-i$, so $|z-i|<|z+i|$, i.e. $|w|<1$. The real axis maps to $|w|=1$.

**57 — (B)** (A) is Liouville's theorem (proved by Cauchy's estimates). (R) is true, but a power series converging everywhere does not by itself give boundedness ⟹ constancy (e.g. $e^z$).

**58 — (B)** $1-\cos z=\frac{z^2}{2}-\frac{z^4}{24}+\dots$, so $f=\frac12-\frac{z^2}{24}+\dots$ has no negative powers: the singularity is removable ($f\to\tfrac12$).

**59 — (D)** $\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{a+b\cos\theta}=\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{a^2-b^2}}$ $(a>|b|)$; here it is $\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{4-1}}=\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt3}$.
